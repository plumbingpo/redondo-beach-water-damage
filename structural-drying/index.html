Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Redondo Beach Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Structural Drying FAQ

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days. The schedule depends on how saturated things are, which materials got wet, the ambient conditions and how the building is constructed. When saturation is heavy or the property is commercial, expect 5 to 7 days or beyond. Daily monitoring lets us keep you informed about the timeline.
Secondary mold most often follows a dry-out attempted with fans alone. Commercial LGR dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential units. Evaporation from the structure is sped up by high-velocity air movers set for precise airflow. Household gear also comes with no way of measuring whether drying has finished.

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
