Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Redondo Beach: What to Know

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Basement Floor Coverings

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Furnace and Water Heater

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.
