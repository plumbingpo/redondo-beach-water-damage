Bathroom Water Damage Help in Redondo Beach

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our Redondo Beach team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

The Room Below

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Leaking Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
