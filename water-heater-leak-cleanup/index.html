Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Redondo Beach

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Redondo Beach, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
